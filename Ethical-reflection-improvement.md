In the modern day, there is a prevalent presence of mobile phones, many people do not even consider leaving the home without one, but why? In the past, before the modern-day phone became available, we used other tools to do the same things—for example, traditional maps, cameras, torches, watches, and MP3 Players. The mobile phone has conveniently replaced them with an all-in-one system; you can thoughtlessly pull out your phone and take a picture or find the nearest gas station without planning. The mobile phone didn't just replace these tools but has improved upon them, putting the definition of convenience as a lightweight small device in your pocket. And as phones have become more popular, the other everyday tools have been phased out, becoming less accessible, and now, in today's modern day, can we truly consider our phones convenient, or something we must own in order to get through daily life? This co-dependency with the mobile phone has led to the amplification of present-day ethical questions that are no longer a simple solution.
